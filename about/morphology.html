Galaxy Morphology

Galaxies in the local Universe fall into a remarkably orderly pattern. A century after Hubble first arranged them along a sequence from smooth spheroids to thin star-forming discs, that sequence remains the organising framework of extragalactic astronomy, and it is far more than a taxonomy: position along it tracks stellar mass, gas content, star formation rate and environment, so morphology and the physics of galaxy growth are bound together. The Hubble sequence is a record of how galaxies assembled, written in their structure.

Reading that record means following it back in time. Deep imaging, and JWST in particular, now resolves galaxy structure into the first few billion years after the Big Bang, and the picture is not simply one of gradual assembly towards the present-day sequence: settled discs, bars and central concentrations appear earlier than the standard picture of hierarchical structure formation had anticipated. Structure therefore emerges rapidly, and the transformations that reshape it – mergers, disc instabilities, the growth of bulges, the fading of discs after quenching – operate throughout cosmic history rather than only at late times.

Connecting those transformations to the sequence we observe requires a model, and here the same difficulty arises as elsewhere in galaxy formation. Cosmological hydrodynamical simulations produce morphologies that broadly resemble real galaxies, but the outcome is the sum of many coupled processes, and reproducing the observed mix of morphological types does not by itself reveal which process made a given galaxy what it is. My approach is to dissect the simulations rather than only compare them to data: I decompose morphological change into its physical channels, and use analytic estimators and semi-analytic models to test whether each channel behaves as the underlying physics predicts. The key questions I address are:

  • What sets the Hubble sequence, and how does it emerge across cosmic time?
  • To what extent is galaxy morphology inherited from the assembly history of the host halo?
  • What physical mechanisms link morphology to the star formation and quenching of galaxies?
