SMBH & AGN

Almost every massive galaxy hosts a supermassive black hole (SMBH) at its centre, and the black hole's mass correlates tightly with the properties of its host. This is the signature of SMBH–galaxy co-evolution, and it runs in both directions: gas funnelled into the galactic centre feeds the growth of the black hole, while AGN feedback from the accreting black hole drives gas back out of the galaxy and can eventually quench its star formation.

That a galaxy formation model needs AGN feedback is by now well established. How black holes actually acquire their mass, and how their feedback couples to the surrounding gas, remains far less clear. The questions I am addressing are:

  • What sets the growth rate of a supermassive black hole?
  • What determines how effectively AGN feedback operates?
  • How do black hole growth and feedback fit into the broader baryon cycle that regulates galaxy growth?
