SMBH & AGN

Almost every massive galaxy hosts a supermassive black hole (SMBH) at its centre, and the black hole's mass correlates tightly with properties of its host galaxy, such as bulge mass and velocity dispersion, even though its direct gravitational influence barely extends beyond the innermost parsecs. This correlation is generally attributed to feedback: as the black hole accretes, it releases energy back into its surroundings, either radiatively, through a radiation-pressure-driven wind in a gas-rich, rapidly accreting quasar mode, or kinetically, through a collimated jet in a hot-halo, low-accretion-rate kinetic mode typical of massive, gas-poor galaxies. This AGN feedback is the leading explanation for why star formation shuts down entirely in the most massive galaxies, since supernova feedback alone cannot expel gas from such a deep gravitational potential well.

The necessity of AGN feedback in a galaxy formation model is well established; how it couples to the multiphase gas of an individual galaxy, and why it quenches some galaxies quickly and others only slowly, is less clear. I use cosmological hydrodynamical simulations to dissect this coupling directly, tracking where kinetic AGN feedback deposits its energy and identifying which structural properties of a galaxy determine its effectiveness.

  • How does kinetic AGN feedback actually couple to the multiphase gas in and around a galaxy?
  • Why does the effectiveness of AGN feedback in quenching star formation depend so strongly on a galaxy's structural properties?
  • How does black hole growth and feedback fit into the broader baryon cycle that regulates galaxy growth?
