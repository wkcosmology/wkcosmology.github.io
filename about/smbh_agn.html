SMBH & AGN

One of the most significant advances in galaxy formation theory over the past two decades concerns the impact of feedback from a growing supermassive black hole (SMBH) — observationally manifested as an active galactic nucleus (AGN) — on the global properties of its host galaxy. The influence runs in both directions. Galaxies and their SMBHs co-evolve: gas in the ISM both supplies star formation and feeds the accreting black hole. The feedback released in turn deposits an enormous quantity of energy into the ISM, expels gas from the galaxy, and suppresses further cooling within the halo, ultimately quenching star formation and starving the SMBH of its own fuel. This co-evolution remains among the most important unresolved questions in galaxy formation and evolution.

The problem spans an extraordinary range of scales, from the megaparsec extent of the galaxy clusters that AGN feedback influences down to the sub-micro-parsec radius of the black hole itself. I therefore adopt an effective approach that does not attempt to resolve the physics near the horizon or within the accretion disc. Instead, I model the growth of the SMBH in terms of the galaxy-scale quantities to which it is most closely coupled, and then the manner in which the ensuing feedback shapes subsequent galaxy evolution, with the aim of identifying the simplest prescriptions capable of reproducing the widest range of observational constraints. The questions I am addressing are:

  • How does SMBH growth correlate with the properties of the host galaxy?
  • What determines how efficiently AGN feedback operates?
  • How do black hole growth and feedback fit within the broader baryon cycle that regulates galaxy growth?
