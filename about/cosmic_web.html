Cosmic Web & Dark matter halo

The large-scale structure (LSS) of our Universe grows from a nearly uniform density field carrying small perturbations. Gravity amplifies these perturbations until they collapse, and because that collapse proceeds anisotropically, it gives rise to the distinct components of the cosmic web: collapse along all three axes yields the nodes, collapse along two yields the filaments, collapse along one yields the sheets, and the regions that collapse along none, their matter drained away by the surrounding structures, remain as voids.

The basic unit of the LSS is the dark matter halo, the virialised object formed by the collapse of dark matter. Its deep gravitational potential fosters the formation and evolution of luminous galaxies, which in turn trace the underlying distribution of haloes. Characterising the connection between haloes and the galaxies they host is therefore essential to cosmology, which relies on galaxies as tracers of the LSS in order to constrain cosmological models and the nature of dark matter and dark energy.

This galaxy–halo connection is multi-faceted. Its primary component is the relation between the two masses, the stellar mass–halo mass relation, which observations and galaxy formation models alike have established securely over the past two decades. Attention has consequently shifted to the higher-order connections between galaxies and their haloes, and to the imprint those connections leave on galaxy clustering — an effect known as galaxy assembly bias.

Given the complexity of the problem, these connections are not readily constrained from observation directly. I therefore begin from simulations, either hydrodynamical galaxy formation simulations or N-body simulations populated with galaxies via semi-analytic or empirical models. This allows me to quantify the connections in a controlled setting and to design observational diagnostics with a proper understanding of the attendant systematics and potential biases. The key questions I address are:

  • How are the assembly, structure, and clustering of dark matter haloes related to each other?
  • How do halo properties beyond halo mass shape the properties of the galaxies?
  • How do observed galaxies trace the underlying density field?
